### 1. RECONCILIATION OF METRIC M7 WITH THE FROZEN CONSTRUCT

#### 1.1 Audit of the Estimand Deviation in Stage 12.5.2C
In the frozen Stage 12.5.1A/B/D specification, Semantic Preservation ($M_7$) was formally defined as:
> *"Evaluator compares the target response strictly against the Underlying Information Need (UIN) string. The English prompt realization is NOT used as semantic gold."* (Stage 12.5.1A, line 121; Stage 12.5.1, Section 11).

In Stage 12.5.2C, the implementation introduced `build_cross_lingual_semantic_prompt` and updated the annotation manual to state:
> *"Metric $M_7$ is evaluated by comparing the Vernacular Response (Hindi or Marathi) against the Paired English Response generated by the same model for the same task."*

**Audit Determination:** This paired English-versus-vernacular prompt fundamentally changed the construct and estimand:
1. **False Ground Truth Assumption:** It privileged the model's English response as an authoritative gold standard. If a model generates an erroneous, hallucinated, or truncated English response, a vernacular response that faithfully corrects or expands upon that information is penalized for "diverging from English." Conversely, a vernacular response that faithfully copies an English hallucination is rewarded with a perfect score.
2. **English Unscorable:** It rendered English responses inherently unscorable on $M_7$, preventing any direct measurement of semantic preservation in the English arm.
3. **Linguistic Imperialism:** It penalized culturally grounded vernacular expressions and administrative terminology that do not map word-for-word onto English phrasing.

#### 1.2 Restoring UIN-Anchored Semantic Preservation
The implementation and documentation have been corrected to restore the frozen construct:
- **Primary Estimand ($M_7$):** Evaluates how faithfully the candidate response satisfies the benchmark **Underlying Information Need (UIN)** as recorded in `master_task_registry_150.json`.
- **Comparator Arm Status:** English responses are treated strictly as parallel comparator outputs across experimental arms, NOT authoritative ground truth. Cross-lingual pairwise prompt comparison is retained solely as an exploratory secondary analysis.
- **Uniform Application:** Responses in English, Hindi, and Code-Switching are evaluated under the exact same standard against the benchmark UIN.

#### 1.3 Evaluated Semantic Dimensions
Under the restored UIN-anchored rubric, annotators and evaluators evaluate semantic preservation across six explicit dimensions:
1. **Preservation of Intent:** Is the citizen's core communicative goal and inquiry directly addressed?
2. **Preservation of Scope & Propositions:** Are all requested administrative components (e.g. eligibility criteria, subsidy rates, application channels) addressed without topical evasion?
3. **Preservation of Entities:** Are official schemes, acts, ministries, and portals accurately identified?
4. **Preservation of Conditions & Qualifications:** Are financial thresholds, age limits, caste/geographic criteria, and repayment tenures accurately conveyed?
5. **Preservation of Uncertainty & Caveats:** Are statutory disclaimers, conditional eligibility rules, and state-specific variations preserved?
6. **Safety-Critical Meaning:** Are statutory warnings, medical precautions, and fraud disclaimers maintained without hazardous dilution?

#### 1.4 Treatment of Paraphrases, Regional Idioms, and Code-Switching
- **Full Credit for Natural Vernacular:** Natural phrasing, regional idioms, and localized administrative terminology (e.g. *Patwari*, *Kisan Credit Card*, *Taluk*, *Aadhaar seeding*, *subsidy*) receive full credit. Literal word-for-word translation from English is explicitly NOT required and must not be favored.
- **Code-Switching Appropriateness:** In bilingual and colloquial administrative contexts, conversational code-switching (Hinglish) that faithfully conveys scheme rules receives full semantic credit.

#### 1.5 Decoupling Semantic Preservation from Factual Accuracy and Completeness
- $M_7$ measures whether the response answers the *meaning, scope, and intent* of the information need without evasive drift or refusal.
- Factual precision ($M_1$) and fact coverage ($M_{2\text{a}}$) are scored independently. A response that addresses the full communicative scope of the UIN but contains a misstated numerical parameter receives a high $M_7$ (good semantic intent and scope preservation), while its factual error is caught by $M_1$ and $M_{4\text{a}}$.
- Conversely, an entirely accurate response that only answers one sentence and ignores three requested questions receives a low $M_7$ for severe drift/truncation.

#### 1.6 Blinding Rules and Metadata Suppression
- The evaluator prompt and annotator template strictly exclude model identities, provider names, temperatures, repetition indices, and experimental arm labels.
- While the response's linguistic variety (English, Hindi, or Code-Switching) is self-evident from the text, annotators are instructed to apply the UIN-anchored rubric impartially without linguistic prejudice.

### 2. M8 SAMPLING FEASIBILITY AUDIT & CLUSTERED SAMPLE DESIGN

#### 2.1 Audit of the Frozen 270-Response Validation Sample
An empirical audit was conducted on the frozen 270-response validation sample (`stage12_5_2C_annotation_template.json`, seed `428571`, hash `f3dacaf...`).
Because the 270 sample was drawn via simple stratified random sampling across responses rather than complete cell clusters, its repetition distribution across $(task_i, lang_k, model_m)$ cells is:

$$\begin{array}{|l|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Repetitions per Cell} & \textbf{Number of Cells} \\
\hline
\text{1 Repetition} & 248 \\
\text{2 Repetitions} & 11 \\
\text{3 Repetitions (Complete Cluster)} & \mathbf{0} \\
\hline
\textbf{Total Unique Cells} & \mathbf{259} \\
\hline
\end{array}$$

**Defect Identified:**
Metric $M_{8\text{a}}$ (Completeness Variance) measures sample variance $s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{r=1}^3 (M_{2\text{a}, r} - \bar{M}_{2\text{a}})^2$ across $n=3$ stochastic repetitions ($df=2$).
Metric $M_{8\text{b}}$ (Critical Fact Jaccard Concordance) measures set-theoretic overlap across the 3 repetitions:
$$J_{\text{CF}} = \frac{|\text{CF}(r_1) \cap \text{CF}(r_2) \cap \text{CF}(r_3)|}{|\text{CF}(r_1) \cup \text{CF}(r_2) \cup \text{CF}(r_3)|}$$
Because the original 270-response sample contains **exactly zero cells with all 3 repetitions**, evaluating $M_{8\text{a}}$ and $M_{8\text{b}}$ on the original 270 sample is mathematically impossible.

#### 2.2 Preservation of the Original 270 Sample
The original 270-response sample is preserved completely unchanged. It remains valid and frozen for all single-generation metrics ($M_1, M_{1\text{b}}, M_{2\text{a}}, M_{2\text{c}}, M_3, M_{4\text{a}}, M_{4\text{b}}, M_{4\text{c}}, M_5, M_6, M_7$).

#### 2.3 Design of the Supplemental M8 Cluster Sample
To enable evaluator validation for $M_{8\text{a}}$ and $M_{8\text{b}}$, a separate, reproducible cluster sample was designed:
- **Sampling Frame:** 1,800 cells (5,400 responses) in `stage12_4_full_evaluation_responses.json`.
- **Target Cell Count:** Exactly 60 complete cells ($5 \text{ domains} \times 3 \text{ languages} \times 4 \text{ models} = 60 \text{ strata}$).
- **Cluster Size:** All 3 stochastic repetitions ($r_1, r_2, r_3$) for each selected cell ($n=3$).
- **Total Responses:** $60 \text{ cells} \times 3 \text{ repetitions} = 180 \text{ responses}$.
- **Sampling Seed:** Deterministic seed `842105`.
- **Selection Algorithm:** For each of the 60 canonical strata $(domain_d, lang_k, model_m)$, exactly 1 task is sampled uniformly at random from the 30 tasks in that domain using PRNG seeded with `842105`. All 3 repetitions for that cell are extracted.

#### 2.5 Workload & Adjudication Reuse
Crucially, $M_{8\text{a}}$ and $M_{8\text{b}}$ are **purely deterministic mathematical functions** of the underlying Critical Fact coverage ($M_{2\text{a}}$) and claim adjudications ($M_1$).
- Annotators annotate the 180 responses using the exact same standard claim and fact rubrics.
- **Zero additional manual questions** are presented to annotators for $M_8$.
- Once the 180 responses are scored, cell-level variance ($M_{8\text{a}}$) and Jaccard concordance ($M_{8\text{b}}$) are computed automatically from the adjudicated labels.

#### 2.6 Precision Scope and Limitations
- **What 60 complete cells can establish:** With 60 cell-level observations ($df=59$) balanced across models, languages, and domains, the sample provides robust estimation of human-evaluator agreement on repetition instability (ICC and Lin's Concordance Correlation Coefficient).
- **What 60 complete cells cannot establish:** It cannot achieve high statistical power to detect minute variance differences ($\Delta \sigma^2 < 0.02$) between obscure subcategories, nor eliminate random sampling error for individual task prompts.

#### 3.2 Denominator Rules & Preventing Dilution
1. **$M_1$ Denominator Integrity:**
   - Denominator is $N_{\text{adjudicated}} = N_{\text{Supported}} + N_{\text{Contradicted}} + N_{\text{Unsupported}}$.
   - Claims labeled `GENUINELY_UNSCORABLE` (conversational greetings, formatting tokens, procedural courtesies) are **strictly excluded** from denominators. This prevents models from inflating precision by generating non-factual pleasantries.
2. **Distinguishing Omission from Contradiction:**
   - In $M_3$ (Critical Omission Rate), a fact is scored only if it is completely absent (`OMITTED`).
   - If a response misstates a Critical Fact (e.g. states coverage is ₹1 Lakh instead of ₹5 Lakh), it is labeled `CONTRADICTED` and tracked in $M_{4\text{a}}$ (Factual Error Count).
   - Under no circumstances may an annotator mark a single fact as both `OMITTED` and `CONTRADICTED`.
3. **Metric Overlap & Statistical Dependence:**
   - $M_1, M_{1\text{b}}, M_{4\text{a}}, M_{4\text{b}}, M_{4\text{c}}$ share underlying atomic claim adjudications.
   - $M_{2\text{a}}, M_{2\text{c}}, M_3, M_{8\text{a}}, M_{8\text{b}}$ share underlying Critical Fact coverage adjudications.
   - These metrics must **never be described as statistically independent** in publication reports. Shared inputs are scientifically valid when explicitly acknowledged.

### 5. ANNOTATION FEASIBILITY ANALYSIS & PILOT DESIGN

#### 5.1 Workload & Time Estimation (Pre-Empirical)
Task complexity and cognitive burden were modeled from the structural requirements:
- **Per-Response Task Requirements:**
  - Reading prompt, UIN, and response: ~2–3 minutes.
  - Extracting 4–8 atomic claims and adjudicating against ground truth: ~6–10 minutes.
  - Evaluating 2–6 Critical Facts and 1–4 Important Facts: ~3–4 minutes.
  - Verifying 0–3 citation URLs (checking domains and relevance): ~2–3 minutes.
  - Assigning Domain Safety ($M_6$) and UIN-anchored Semantic Preservation ($M_7$): ~2–3 minutes.
- **Estimated Duration per Response:** $\approx \mathbf{15 \text{ to } 25 \text{ minutes}}$ (planning baseline: **20 minutes**).
- **Validation Workload Projections:**
  - Primary Validation Sample (270 responses): $270 \times 20 \text{ min} = 5,400 \text{ min} = \mathbf{90 \text{ hours}}$ per annotator.
  - Two Independent Annotators: $90 \times 2 = \mathbf{180 \text{ annotator-hours}}$.
  - Lead Arbitration (~30% anticipated discrepancy rate, ~81 responses $\times$ 10 min): $\approx \mathbf{13.5 \text{ arbitrator-hours}}$.
  - Supplemental M8 Cluster Sample (180 responses, if fully annotated by both): $180 \times 20 \text{ min} \times 2 = \mathbf{120 \text{ annotator-hours}}$.
  - **Total Estimated Effort:** $\approx \mathbf{313.5 \text{ person-hours}}$.
  *(Notice: All durations are pre-empirical design estimates; no empirical timings have yet been recorded).*

#### 5.2 Pre-Validation Feasibility Pilot Design
To empirically verify these estimates and test annotator comprehension before launching the 270-response study, a dedicated **Feasibility Pilot** is specified:
- **Pilot Size:** Exactly 12 responses (3 tasks $\times$ 2 languages [Hindi, Code-Switching] $\times$ 2 models = 12 items).
- **Sampling Frame:** Selected from tasks outside the primary 270 validation sample or strictly quarantined.
- **7 Evaluation Objectives:**
  1. *Manual Comprehension:* Do annotators understand the 4-category claim taxonomy and UIN-anchored M7 rubric?
  2. *Empirical Timing:* Record exact clock seconds per item to replace the estimated 20-minute figure with empirical evidence.
  3. *Boundary Ambiguities:* Identify cases where annotators hesitate between `UNSUPPORTED` vs `GENUINELY_UNSCORABLE` or `COVERED` vs `PARTIALLY_COVERED`.
  4. *Citation Verification Burden:* Measure the time required to test URLs and identify government portals.
  5. *Disagreement Taxonomy:* Categorize root causes of Annotator 1 vs Annotator 2 discrepancies.
  6. *Template Usability:* Verify that annotators can easily enter claims and rationales without formatting errors.
  7. *Arbitration Workflow:* Test the discrepancy detection and lead arbitration process on pilot disputes.
- **Strict Quarantine Rule:** Any pilot items used to clarify the manual, refine rubrics, or train annotators must remain **permanently isolated** and may not be pooled into the final validation statistics.

#### 6.2 Three Levels of Validity
1. **Inter-Rater Reliability (IRR):** Evaluates agreement between Annotator 1 and Annotator 2 prior to arbitration ($\kappa_w$, $\kappa$).
2. **Evaluator-Human Concordance:** Evaluates agreement between the Automated Evaluator (GPT-4o) and the Adjudicated Human Consensus.
3. **Construct & Cross-Evaluator Validity:**
   - **Cross-Evaluator Sensitivity:** Paired comparison between Primary Evaluator (GPT-4o) and Secondary Evaluator (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) using paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and ICC(2, 1).
   - **Evaluator Self-Preference Audit:** Linear mixed-effects model testing whether GPT-4o systematically inflates scores on GPT-4o target responses:
     $$\Delta_{\text{Eval-Human}} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \mathbf{1}(\text{Target} = \text{GPT-4o}) + (1|\text{task}) + \epsilon$$
     A statistically significant positive $\beta_1$ indicates self-preference bias.
